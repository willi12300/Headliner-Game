# Headliner Derived Values Specification V1

## Governing rule

Stored facts and career history generate derived values. Major derived values must never be independently randomised. Each calculation returns both a result and a component breakdown used by Developer Mode.

## Band calculation chain

People skills, personality, health and relationships → band fundamentals → career outcomes → market state → commercial outcomes.

### Overall quality (0–100)

- Catalogue quality: 30%
- Role-specific member skill: 25%
- Live performance: 20%
- Chemistry: 15%
- Creative direction: 10%

### Growth ceiling (0–100)

- Member potential: 55%
- Songwriting: 20%
- Ambition: 15%
- Creativity: 10%

The growth ceiling may never be lower than overall quality.

### Live performance (0–100)

- Role-specific skill: 35%
- Stage presence: 30%
- Chemistry: 20%
- Health and availability: 15%

### Catalogue quality (0–100)

- Songwriting: 55%
- Creativity: 30%
- Musicianship: 15%

### Popularity (0–100)

- Career exposure: 35%
- Recent success: 25%
- Commercial appeal: 20%
- Momentum: 10%
- Regional reach: 10%

### Festival draw

Festival draw score combines popularity, live performance, fan loyalty, momentum and festival suitability. Raw ticket draw is produced from a nonlinear curve, so high-level acts separate meaningfully from mid-card acts.

### Followers

Followers are produced from a nonlinear popularity curve modified by commercial conversion and regional reach. They cannot be rolled separately.

### Booking fee

Booking fee is produced from festival draw, reputation and momentum. The fee breakdown is retained for inspection.

## Randomness

Randomness creates people, career contexts and event variance. It may influence causes, but it must not independently overwrite their derived outcomes.
